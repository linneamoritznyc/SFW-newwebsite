How we’re governed, and where the money goes

The Soil Food Web Foundation is a 501(c)(3) public charity. It holds that status in its own name, so it no longer needs a fiscal sponsor and every donation goes directly to the Foundation.

Charity status

Soil Food Web Foundation is a nonprofit public benefit corporation, incorporated in October 2025 and recognized by the IRS as a 501(c)(3) public charity in 2026. EIN 39-4439236. Registered office: 5441 S Macadam Ave Ste N, Portland, Oregon 97239.

Because the Foundation holds that status itself, it takes donations directly. Nothing passes through a fiscal sponsor, and no third party takes a cut on the way.

IRS determination letter, to be posted below.

The exact date on the determination letter, so this paragraph can name a month rather than a year. Decision 9. Evan.

What came before

The Soil Food Web School was a for-profit business. It is now the Foundation’s education program and is run by the Foundation.

That change matters for two reasons. Course fees are program revenue: after the cost of running the courses they pay for scholarships, open research and field projects. And payments for courses, workshops and programs are fees for services, so they are not tax-deductible charitable gifts. Donations are.

Course fees from March to the middle of September 2026 came to $419,844.

Foundation revenue records. Figures supplied by the Foundation, September 2026.

Money, plainly

The Foundation has two kinds of income. Course fees, which are payments for a service. Donations, which are gifts.

Donations are processed through Givebutter, which charges the Foundation no transaction fee. A dollar given is a dollar received.

What each kind of income paid for over the year will be set out in the impact report in December 2026. Until that report exists, this page will not print a breakdown, because a split nobody has audited is worth nothing to a reader.

What the money did

The clearest single thing it did is scholarships. In June 2026 alone the Foundation awarded more than $72,000 in full scholarships and extended more than $250,000 in partial ones.
