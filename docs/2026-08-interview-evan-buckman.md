## 4. Elaine Ingham and the shape of what was inherited

- Elaine effectively defined the scientific category. When she started, the claims she made — that soil biology matters to plant health, that microbes are not merely a nuisance — were considered fringe.
- She published around eighty papers. Evan notes with visible irritation that the site's publications page is an unlinked bibliography, many files apparently missing, and that nobody thought to link her Google Scholar profile at the top.
- Her credibility rested on proof: she could point to a specific four-thousand-hectare farm and describe doubling yields in the first year.
- **Evan's own caveat, unprompted:** not everyone who takes her classes is her. People get hyped and then don't get comparable results, because she refined her methods over decades. He also names "cherry picking and marketing" as part of the mix. This is important — he already sees the overclaiming problem without being told.
- The ecosystem design was coherent: projects generate data → data feeds research → research feeds education → education builds a workforce → workforce implements projects.
- She had a corporation and an LLC, with **two separate sets of business partners and two separate sets of books**. The school was extraordinarily successful and highly profitable. The consulting side she ran to help people, not as a business — she was, in his words, the furthest thing from a business person. Mismatch plus inter-partner conflict and drama.
- One business partner is described as shady and grimy, and the organisation's reputation deteriorated accordingly.
- Elaine had Lewy body dementia. When Evan first met her she retained some clarity but was already years in. She declined sharply and died in February 2026.
- Evan spent the last few years working with Elaine, her family and her team on the transition plan.

## 7. The India opportunity

The strategic centre of the conversation.

- **Origin.** At Climate Week in New York, in a boardroom with heads of UNDP, the FAO, major Brazilian holding companies and African dignitaries. Evan arrived believing he held insider knowledge — that biology and microbes were a secret that could unlock sustainable profitability — and expected to have to convince the room. He realised they were already moving fast in that direction. His conclusion, which underpins everything since: **the world is convinced the data is there and knows what to do, but still doesn't know how to do it.** The gap is implementation, not awareness. He describes the state of the conversation as immature, and sees decades of work in advancing it.
- Route in: the founder of Nexus started the **Amazon Investor Coalition**, on the premise that a regenerative bioeconomy can outcompete the deforestation economy — açaí worth more than deforested soy and beef. Evan said yes when asked to help and spent about two years on it. There he met **Paula**, then head of agriculture and environment at **Pegasus Capital**, one of the largest and oldest impact investment firms, which signs cheques into Green Climate Fund and UN sustainability financing. Evan flagged that Linnea seems suited to navigating international finance bureaucracy.
- At that convening he heard **Vijay Kumar** present.

**Vijay Kumar and APCNF**
- A career government worker who encountered Elaine's work and the soil food web, and acted on the premise that caring for soil removes the need for expensive fertiliser.
- Built the programme on existing **women's social and economic support groups** — women earning under a dollar a day pooling resources into community funds, borrowing for village-improving equipment, receiving preferential bank rates because their repayment rates are so high.
- **The distinguishing feature:** it's the only such programme in India that does not subsidise farmers for switching. No incentive payments. Results speak for themselves.
- Farmers make their own biofertilisers and biostimulants, and plant polyculture food forests rather than commodity monocrops for export — so they eat year-round from their own land.
- Incomes have risen roughly 5x.
- Andhra Pradesh is historically one of the world's most devastated regions from industrial agriculture and the Green Revolution.
- Ground temperature between converted and adjacent fields can differ by around forty degrees, measurable with a temperature gun. Visible from space — barren landscape with green squares of forest.
- Scale as Evan stated it: close to two million farmers, about 30% of the region's farmers, on track for 100% within ten years. Roughly a billion dollars mobilised, which works out to about USD 500 per farmer.
- **The fertiliser subsidy deal.** The Indian government is the world's largest fertiliser purchaser, because it subsidises for farmers. RySS negotiated that half the money the government *doesn't* spend on fertiliser for enrolled farmers comes to them in cash. About USD 150 million received, implying roughly USD 300 million saved in subsidies over fifteen years.
- Comparison point Evan offers: Gambia spends about 30% of its entire government budget buying fertiliser for smallholders.
- Evan calls it the coolest project he's aware of in the space. Everyone called them crazy for a decade.
- **They cite Elaine and the soil food web as their primary scientific inspiration** — the body of work Evan now stewards. He describes this as a dream come true.

**The proposal**
- Soil Food Web's differentiator is direct microscopy — actually looking at soil ecology under a microscope. RySS doesn't do this. Their biostimulants are made differently, but the underlying basis is the same.
- Evan pitched adding a soil microscopy **data layer** to APCNF's existing work as a long-term research collaboration. They were enthusiastic.
- He also wants to get some of their staff to the October workshop.

**The October workshop**
- Two-week condensed version of the advanced programme — group learning instead of solo trial and error.
- Long-running in Costa Rica and England; recently run in Santa Fe, New Mexico for the first time.
- **India is the first in Asia**, co-hosted with the **Isha Foundation / Sadhguru's Save Soil**, which claims a billion-person reach and runs a policy arm advising governments and participating in COP gatherings.
- Evan travels early October.

**Sri Lanka as the cautionary tale**
- Sri Lanka outlawed agricultural chemicals and went fully organic statewide. It failed badly because there was no transition plan.
- Evan's framing: soils and plants are addicted to the chemicals, so going cold turkey produces withdrawal. The organic movement is defined by what you *don't* do, not what you *do*.
- **This is the core Soil Food Web pitch:** natural recovery takes three to ten years depending on starting conditions. Incubating beneficial biology separately and reapplying it compresses that to one to three years.
- Sikkim is offered as the counter-example that worked.
- Elaine's Sri Lanka projects — including outfits growing 400,000 hectares of sugar cane — are given as an example of overpromising: fifteen years later they were still finishing what they'd started.

## 10. Verification notes

Checked after the call. Both matter because they'd otherwise propagate into a proposal.

1. **The prize is not the World Food Prize.** Evan said "they just won the World Food Prize." APCNF won the **2026 Food Planet Prize** — USD 1.5 million from Sweden's **Curt Bergfors Foundation**, presented in Båstad, Sweden on 2 June 2026. Vijay Kumar Thallam and Special Chief Secretary Rajsekhar Budithi accepted. APCNF was also a joint winner of the €1 million **Gulbenkian Prize for Humanity** in 2024, and Vijay Kumar received the M.S. Swaminathan Award for Environment Protection in 2023.

2. **Scale figures, confirmed and refined.** 1.8 million farm families, 700,000 farmworker families, 340,000 women's self-help groups, 8,000+ villages. Began with 40,000 smallholders in 2016. Target is all ~6 million farming households in Andhra Pradesh — sources give 2035.

3. **RySS structure.** Rythu Sadhikara Samstha — "Farmer Empowerment Organisation" — is a not-for-profit **fully owned by the Government of Andhra Pradesh**, nested in the Department of Agriculture and Cooperation, established by G.O. Rt No. 764, 10 November 2016. Not an NGO in the ordinary sense, which is what makes the fertiliser-subsidy arrangement possible.

4. **Vijay Kumar Thallam.** IAS 1983, 40+ years in government. Ten years as CEO of the Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty, mobilising 11.5 million rural poor women into self-help groups — so the SHG network predated the farming programme and was pointed at it. Vice Chair (Production) of the Champions Network for the 2021 UN Food Systems Summit. Now championing replication in 22 other Indian states plus Zambia and Sri Lanka.

5. **Delivery model.** APCNF runs on **master farmers** — practitioners who train 100–150 neighbours each. Women are over 60% of them. Peer-to-peer extension, structurally different from Soil Food Web's certified-consultant model. Worth thinking about which model any collaboration is meant to serve.

6. **Language.** Telugu is the official language of Andhra Pradesh, spoken by roughly 89% of the population. But RySS leadership are English-fluent career civil servants — Telugu serves farmers and master farmers, not the proposal audience.
