What we test before we recommend

  • Measurable. Every recommendation starts with a count, not a guess.
  • Diagnostic first. We look at your soil before we suggest anything.
  • Compatible. No need to tear up what you have.

Each mechanism we teach is established soil biology. How much of any one benefit a field sees depends on climate, crop, starting condition and management. We publish results by site, with the person and the year, and say what one site cannot prove.

Practice

It works on real land, in the hands of real growers.

The biology is universal; the practice is local. Every number we publish carries a name, a place and a year.

Nick Padwick farms four thousand acres in Norfolk, England. Roberto Silva works a hundred and forty in Piura, Peru. Jenn Sirp grows on a quarter acre in California. Eleven of them have filmed their own work and published it: where they started, what they changed, and what was measured after, at length and in their own words.

The scale runs from that quarter acre to ten thousand acres of corn and soy in Illinois, and a public park on Governors Island in New York. The method does not change with the acreage. Assess the biology under a microscope, make the amendment that is missing, apply it, and count again.

Community

A global community makes this work outlast any one of us.

Dr. Ingham built the method to be handed on. Nothing in it depends on her being in the room: it is a microscope, a set of counts anyone can learn to read, and people trained to read them. Soil Regenerators on six continents now teach it to their neighbors, assess their own ground, and publish what they find.

That is what outlasts any one of us. Every role in it is open.
