Advanced Programs

Our story

It began with one scientist looking closer.

Dr. Elaine Ingham pioneered research in soil biology across four decades and made her science radically accessible, so that anyone anywhere could learn to restore the soil beneath their feet.

She founded this work; the Foundation was created in 2025 to carry it forward. She passed in February 2026, and the work she started continues through the Foundation, the school inside it, and practitioners in more than 100 countries who bring the same close attention to their own soil.

She taught at Oregon State from 1986 to 2002, served as the first President of the Soil Ecology Society, and co-authored the USDA's Soil Biology Primer.

Why this matters for climatePlants pull carbon from the air and invest much of it in the soil, where it is built into microbial bodies, aggregates and long-lived fungal networks. Biological management also avoids emissions: less fertilizer manufactured and moved, fewer passes over the field.

The ground beneath our farms and forests is alive.

A web of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes and micro-arthropods feeds plants, builds soil, stores carbon and cleans water when it is intact.

For four decades Dr. Elaine Ingham mapped that web and taught the world to restore it. We carry her work forward: teaching the science to anyone willing to learn, opening the research for the world to test, proving the practice on real land, and growing a global community of Soil Regenerators. Living soil is the solution, and it is beneath your feet.
