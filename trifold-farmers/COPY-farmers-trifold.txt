2. FIRST FLAP

WHY BIOLOGY

The soil beneath your farm is alive.

[Photograph] Dr. Elaine Ingham at the microscope.

A web of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods feeds plants, builds soil, stores carbon, and cleans water *when it is intact*.

What biologically complete, properly-structured soil does:

CYCLES NUTRIENTS
CREATES SOIL STRUCTURE
INHIBITS WEED PRESSURE
REDUCES PESTS AND DISEASE

For forty-five years, Dr. Elaine Ingham mapped the Soil Food Web and taught the world to restore it. Today we train farmers, and the people who work with them, to move from chemical inputs to biology.


3. INSIDE LEFT

MICROSCOPY

See what lives in your soil.

[Photograph] Testate amoeba, 40x objective. Photograph: Joy Kaluf.

Every recommendation starts with a biological analysis under a microscope. You'll learn to take a microbial count, which names what is present in your soil or compost sample and their relative numbers: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods.

The count shows you what your soil is missing. After you add it, you measure again to confirm microbial counts are going in the expected directions.

You can learn this in our Soil Microscopy program, which also prepares you for a career in soil biology analysis as an SFW Lab-Technician.


4. INSIDE MIDDLE

COMPOSTING AND LIQUID AMENDMENTS

Make your own biology on your farm.

[Photograph] A workshop group around a finished compost pile.

The basic methodology is universal; the application is based upon locally-sourced materials. You'll learn to produce high-quality BioComplete™ compost and biological liquid amendments, and to apply them to rebalance the microbial content of your soil.

You can learn this in our BioComplete™ Compost Production and Biological Liquid Amendments and Field Trial programs. You can also join an Accelerator Workshop, an immersive, two-week, hands-on intensive where you produce compost and liquid amendments live, on a partner's land.


5. INSIDE RIGHT

HOLISTIC MANAGEMENT

Growers are already implementing the Soil Food Web Approach at every scale.

[Photograph] Agroecology plots at Rancho Cacachilas.

Holistic management means planning your whole operation around the biology in your soil. We help you convert your current operations to the Soil Food Web Approach, at any scale.

Nick Padwick farms four thousand acres in Norfolk, England. Roberto Silva works a hundred and forty in Piura, Peru. Jenn Sirp grows on a quarter acre in California. Each of them describes how the Soil Food Web Approach changed their operation by reducing inputs, creating better outputs, and improving their bottom line.
