2. OUR STORY  [inside, left]

OUR STORY

It began with one scientist looking closer.

[Photograph] Dr. Elaine Ingham at the microscope.

The Soil Food Web Foundation was established in October 2025 to carry forward the life's work of Dr. Elaine Ingham, the scientist whose research transformed our understanding of soil biology and its central role in food production, ecosystem health, and planetary regeneration.

She passed away in February 2026, and the work she started continues through the Foundation, the school that is a part of it, and Soil Food Web practitioners in more than 132 countries, who are helping farmers restore the world's soils.

From 1996 onward, Dr. Ingham developed new methods of rapidly assessing soil and foliage-related organisms and researched how soil and foliar biology changes with different management practices on farms. From 2011 to 2014, Dr. Ingham served as the Chief Scientist for the Rodale Institute in Pennsylvania, one of the leading organic farming research centers, before founding the Soil Food Web School in 2019. That school has grown into the nonprofit Soil Food Web Foundation.

3. TEACHING  [outside, left: the fold-in panel]

TEACHING

Learn to grow your own soil biology.

[Photograph] A mentor teaching on working land.

From first curiosity to professional practice. Everyone starts with the Foundation Courses. Where you go next depends on whether you want to apply your skills to your own land or help other farmers improve their soils.

Foundation Courses
The science of the soil food web, the organisms and their roles, and how biological amendments are made and applied. Online, self-paced, about 150 hours, and required before any Advanced Program.

Advanced Programs
Soil Microscopy, BioComplete™ Compost Production, Biological Liquid Amendments and Field Trial, each with its own mentor. All four together are the full path.

Accelerator Workshops
Immersive, two-week, hands-on intensives where participants build the practical skills and confidence to create and apply soil biology principles in real-world settings through the live production of BioComplete™ compost and biological liquid amendments. Held periodically, and hosted with a partner on their land. Dates and places go up on the school site.

Where it leads
Careers in soil biology analysis as SFW Lab-Technicians, working with farmers and land stewards to implement the Soil Food Web Approach as SFW Consultants, and producing high-quality BioComplete™ Compost as SFW Compost Producers.

5. PRACTICE  [inside, right: the fold-in panel]

PRACTICE

Growers are already implementing the Soil Food Web Approach at every scale.

[Photograph] Agroecology plots at Rancho Cacachilas.

The basic methodology is universal; the application is based upon locally-sourced materials. Our case studies and field trials demonstrate the efficacy across a range of soil types at every scale from market gardens to massive industrial farming operations.

Nick Padwick farms four thousand acres in Norfolk, England. Roberto Silva works a hundred and forty in Piura, Peru. Jenn Sirp grows on a quarter acre in California. Check out their individual accounts of how the Soil Food Web Approach changed their operations through reducing inputs, creating better outputs and improving their bottom lines.

Though the projects run from backyard gardens to expansive public parks, the same process is applicable. The steps are the same at every scale: look at the biology under a microscope, add what is missing to rebalance the microbial content, then measure again to confirm microbial counts are going in the expected directions.

We partner with growers to teach them microscopy and biological compost creation and application so they can implement the approach where they farm, and document the impacts.
